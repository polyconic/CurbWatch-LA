Terms & data notes

What CurbWatch LA knows, how it knows it, and where it stops.

The posted sign is the authority. This map is not. Use it to plan, then read the curb. If the two disagree, the sign wins, and a parking officer will agree with the sign.

What this is

A free, independent map of street-parking rules and enforcement for part of Los Angeles, built from public records. It isn't affiliated with, endorsed by, or run by the City of Los Angeles, LADOT, the City of West Hollywood, or any other agency or company.

Where the data comes from

  • City of Los Angeles — posted street-sweeping routes, street centerlines, metered parking inventory and live meter occupancy, city-owned lots, and parking citations.
  • City of West Hollywood — permit blocks and their posted hours, permit districts, sweeping routes, and public parking.
  • County of Los Angeles — address points and city boundaries.
  • OpenStreetMap — streets, parks, off-street lots, and plate-reader camera locations. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Those parts of the data file are a derivative database and stay under that licence — see DATA-LICENSE.md.
  • Dodger Stadium home dates — kept by hand from the published schedule.

It's a snapshot, and it ages

The map carries a build date and the date its ticket records run through. Nothing updates by itself between builds. Schedules change, districts get added, meters get repriced, and enforcement patterns shift after a neighbourhood complaint. An old snapshot stays confidently wrong, which is why the date is on the screen.

Some of it is inferred, not official

Los Angeles hasn't published a permit-district map since 2015, and that map no longer matches the street. So permit streets here are worked out from where permit tickets were actually written, and the Dodger Stadium game-day zone is assembled the same way, from ticket patterns and local knowledge. These are this project's inferences, labelled as such in the app. They can be wrong in both directions: a permit street nobody has ticketed yet looks identical to a free one.

What it can't see at all

No dataset covers red curbs, hydrants, driveways, loading zones, temporary no-parking signs for filming or construction, valet hours, or a sweeper that didn't show up. Ticket history describes the past; it doesn't promise anything about today.

No warranty

This is provided as is, without warranty of any kind. Parking decisions are yours, and so are any citations, tows or fees that follow. Nothing here is legal advice. If you're contesting a citation, LADOT's Initial Review process and the posted signage are what matter.

Privacy

No accounts, no tracking, no analytics, no advertising, no cookies. Nothing you search or click is sent anywhere or recorded. Your chosen light or dark theme is stored in your own browser and never leaves it.

The page makes one live request, to the city's public meter-occupancy feed, so open spaces show current readings. That request comes from your browser to the City of Los Angeles and carries nothing about you beyond what any web request carries. If it fails, the map falls back to the readings in the snapshot.

The citation data behind this map is aggregated by block before publishing. It contains no licence plates, vehicle details, ticket numbers or anything identifying a person — only counts, violation types, times and fine amounts.

Plate-reader cameras

Camera locations come from OpenStreetMap, mapped by volunteers, the same public data DeFlock displays. Coverage is incomplete, and a camera's absence here means nobody has mapped it, not that it isn't there. Manufacturer names are shown to describe the hardware, nothing more.

Corrections

Found a block that's wrong? That's useful — the gap between what's published and what's on the curb is the whole point of this project.
